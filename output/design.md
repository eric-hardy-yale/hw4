# Design — Problem 9

Goal: the site should feel like a real Yale shop on Broadway, not a class demo, so shoppers trust it, browse longer, and reach a product page.

| Area | What changed | Why it should help customers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts** | Libre Baskerville for headings, a collegiate varsity face (Graduate) for the logo and small labels, Inter for body text. All are self-hosted through `@fontsource`, so there are no third-party font requests | Serif plus varsity type reads like a Yale outfitter right away. Inter keeps prices and descriptions easy to scan. Self-hosting means fast loads and no tracking |
| **Color** | Yale blue (#00356b) with a darker navy, a light "sky" accent, and a warm off-white page background instead of plain white. Product photos sit on white cards. Stock badges use green, amber and red | Stays on-brand without being all one blue. The warm background makes the white product photos pop. Badge colors say "in stock / some sizes gone / almost gone" before anyone reads the words |
| **Hierarchy** | Home has a clear order: hero (headline, two buttons, trust line "Officially licensed · Live stock · 57 Broadway") → **Find your fit** category tiles with real photos and "From $32" prices → assistant promo band → **New Haven staples** product row → family and store callout. Product pages have breadcrumbs, a big name and price, then colors → size → actions | Every screen has one obvious next step. Starting prices on category tiles answer "can I afford this?" early, and the trust line answers "is this real?" |
| **Product presentation** | Hero collage of four real products. Cards with soft shadows, image zoom on hover, a "View details →" reveal, stock badges and a sizes line. Product page: cursor-following image zoom, color swatches, a **size picker** (sold-out sizes crossed out, "Hurry, only 5 left in XXL"), **Ask the assistant about this**, and a **You might also like** row (same ranking as the agent's `find_similar`) | Shoppers can see the print up close, know right away whether their size exists, and always have another item to click. That keeps them browsing instead of leaving at a sold-out size |
| **Chat feel** | Branded "Y" avatar on the button, header and each assistant message, a green "online" dot, a pulsing dot on the chat button until first opened, a panel that springs up, animated typing dots, bubbles that fade in, and Markdown replies with mini product cards. The chat can be opened from Home ("Try it") and from product pages with a question already filled in | It feels like talking to a store employee, not a form. The pulse and the in-page buttons get more shoppers into the chat, which is the fastest way from "do you have this in M?" to a product card |
| **Motion** | Subtle page fade-in, cards that lift on hover, the hero collage floats in, and chat results drop in with a stagger. Everything is turned off for `prefers-reduced-motion` | Motion signals "this is live and responsive" (especially when the chat puts cards on the page) without slowing anyone down, and it respects shoppers who get motion sick |
| **Mobile** | The nav collapses to a Menu button, the hero stacks, size buttons fall to 3 per row, the chat panel fits the screen, and there's no sideways scroll at 375 px | Many shoppers are students on phones walking past the store |
